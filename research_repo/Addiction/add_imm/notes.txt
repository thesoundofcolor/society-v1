Addiction → Immigration = -0.04

Negligible, at best, influence.

If Addiction rises, Immigration slightly decreases through reduced legal admissibility, increased deportation risk, and lower long-term settlement stability. The relationship is, however, weak and indirect, as immigration flows are mostly driven by economic opportunity, policy, and geopolitical conditions rather than substance use patterns.

Addiction influences immigration outcomes by affecting legal status and system interaction. Substance-related offenses, health evaluations, or general instability can reduce eligibility for entry, residency, or citizenship. Additionally, addiction can impair integration and long-term settlement success. That being said, I could only find a limited number of relevant articles on the subject, so I cannot confidently argue for a stronger or weaker effect.

In the most thorough resource available in the repo ('Addiction-2022-DeFries-Immigration status matters.pdf'), immigration status is shown to more strongly shape addiction risk and outcomes than the reverse, indicating this edge is secondary and structurally constrained relative to the primary direction: Immigration → Addiction.
