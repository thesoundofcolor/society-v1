Notes for this influence:

Addiction → Military = -0.16

Addiction directly decreases military effectiveness; In studies, substance dependence reduces recruitment eligibility, degrades readiness, and impairs operational performance, while increasing the medical burden and long-term stress on personnel and military support systems. (See: s12954-022-00652-2.pdf and s40352-021-00134-w.pdf)

***Shaping:***

1) Linear (consistent)
Very reasonable. Military effectiveness declines evenly as addiction increases; even small increases reduce eligibility and performance across the force, with compounding effects on readiness and reliability.

2) Sigmoid (threshold)
At low levels, military systems absorb the impact through screening, discipline, and medical oversight. Once addiction reaches a critical threshold within the population, recruitment pools shrink more noticeably and readiness declines more rapidly as disqualification rates and internal strain increase.

3) Cubed Monotopic (realistic)
The impact of addiction on military is shaped by interacting variables such as healthcare variables, mental health, poverty, education, and/or others. These interactions can either buffer early effects or accelerate degradation, producing nonlinear shifts in force readiness and long-term capability.
