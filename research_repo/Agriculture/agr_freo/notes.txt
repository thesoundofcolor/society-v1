Notes for this influence:

Agriculture → Freedom of Speech = -0.08


Agriculture does not inherently suppress freedom of speech; in smaller grassroots agrarian systems, decentralized communities often allow for more local autonomy and informal expression. However, as agriculture industrializes, it introduces strong incentives for information control. Large-scale operations, corporate consolidation, and biosecurity concerns create pressure to limit transparency and manage public perception. This leads to policies and legal frameworks that restrict free speech; including protest, whistleblowing, and investigative access (especially around animal welfare and food production practices). 

The result is a slight but noticeable downward pressure on free and open expression, not from agriculture itself, but from the systems built around it.

Small, consistent downward pressure
No strong thresholds; effect scales gradually with industrialization

2) Monotonic cubic
Very weak early effect
Picks up slightly as agriculture becomes centralized/industrial
Still capped low; this is a small influence overall
