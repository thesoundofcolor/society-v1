Notes for this influence:

Agriculture → Immigration = +0.13

This is a moderate positive labor influence. Strong agricultural systems often create demand for migrant labor, especially where domestic labor supply is insufficient or structurally unavailable.

Agriculture does not directly determine immigration levels on its own, since immigration is heavily filtered through policy, borders, legal frameworks, and broader economic conditions. However, agriculture can still act as a meaningful pull factor by generating persistent labor demand.

The realized strength of this edge should be strongly moderated by higher-order factors, since agriculture may create the demand for immigration without guaranteeing that inflow occurs.
