Notes for this influence:

Agriculture → Welfare (Government Support) = -0.12

This is a small direct decrease but is measurable and empirically relevant. Agriculture reduces general welfare demand through food affordability and rural income, but the effect is diluted across the full population; where welfare is driven more by broader economic and policy factors. Direct impact exists but is limited and partially captured elsewhere.

## Shaping:

1) Polynomial (blended)
- Weak at first → stronger at mid-range → tapers off at upper region

2) Saturating (negative)
- Early drop → diminishing returns

Optional: Asymmetry - Declines in agriculture increases welfare faster than agricultural gains reduce it
** Asymmetry may introduce extreme drift, so if there isn't an automatic corrective drift, this may cause exponential run-off leading to maxing out (in lower or upper levels, where the value goes to zero or 100%)
