Notes for this influence:

Agriculture → Sickness/Disease = +0.18

Agricultural activity does positively influence the burden of sickness and disease. Through both pathogen exposure and environmental health stressors ( like: bioaerosols, particulates, ammonia, etc.) respiratory and infectious disease risk at the population level in increased. Effects are strongest in high-intensity systems and in nearby populations.

Intensive farming emits bioaerosols (bacteria, fungi, endotoxins) that are empirically linked to respiratory illness and asthma-like symptoms. Other agricultural emissions (especially ammonia) contribute to chemical formations which are associated to causing COPD, lung disease, cancers, and other sickness and diseases.

Livestock proximity increases exposure to zoonotic diseases and respiratory conditions in both workers and nearby communities

Counterbalance:
Early-life exposure to farm environments can reduce some atopic conditions (hygiene hypothesis), slightly dampening the upper-end severity
