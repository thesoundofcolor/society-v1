Notes for this influence:

Agriculture → Employment = +0.21

Direct influence. Agriculture is a primary driver of employment. Especially so in rural economies; generating labor demand across production, processing, and supply chains. As agricultural activity expands or shifts toward higher-value and more intensive systems, labor demand tends to increase in tandem. 

Empirical evidence (in agr_emp repo) shows that certain agricultural advancements (e.g., greenhouse or organic systems) can increase total labor demand, particularly by enabling year-round production and requiring more hands-on work.

While mechanization and large-scale efficiency can reduce labor needs at higher levels, offsetting the higher end of growth, agriculture overall remains a net employment engine, with especially strong effects in labor-intensive and developing contexts.

***Shaping***
- Notes:
	- Lower range: steady baseline employment (subsistence, small-scale)
	- Mid range: strong growth (intensification, diversification, value chains)
	- Upper range: softening (automation, efficiency gains)

1) Monotonic (cubic)
- greatly benefits from higher-order influence
- Strong rise early; slows as efficiency and scale reduce marginal labor demand
- Best overall fit

2) Logistic (saturated high-end)
- Employment grows, then levels off as systems mature
- Slightly cleaner, but a bit too constrained

3) Linear
- Simple, but too blunt for such a direct and strong pathway
- Assumes constant job creation, which isn’t realistic
- Easiest to implement, maybe use for initial tuning?
